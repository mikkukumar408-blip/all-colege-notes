### 1. In-Depth Mathematical Foundations of Matrix Rank

> 🟢 **Rigorous Linear Algebra Definition:** Let $A$ be an $m \times n$ matrix with entries in $\mathbb{R}$ (or $\mathbb{C}$). The **Rank** of $A$, denoted by $\rho(A)$ or $\text{rank}(A)$, is defined as the dimension of the vector space spanned by its row vectors (Row Rank) or column vectors (Column Rank). By the Fundamental Rank Theorem of Linear Algebra:
> $$\mathbf{\text{Row Rank}(A) = \text{Column Rank}(A) = \rho(A) \le \min(m, n)}$$

#### Minor-Based Criterion of Rank:
A matrix $A$ is said to be of rank $r$ if and only if:
1. There exists **at least one minor** (determinant of a square submatrix) of order $r$ that is strictly non-zero: $|M_r| \ne 0$.
2. **Every minor** of order $(r + 1)$ and higher vanishes identically: $|M_{r+1}| = 0$.

---

### 2. Deep-Dive: Row Echelon Form Reduction

#### Strict Mathematical Criteria for Row Echelon Form:
A matrix $E$ is in **Row Echelon Form** if:
1. **Zero Rows at Bottom:** All rows consisting entirely of zeros are grouped at the very bottom.
2. **Leading Entry (Pivot):** The leading coefficient (the first non-zero entry from the left) of each non-zero row is strictly to the right of the leading coefficient of the row directly above it.
3. **Zeros Below Pivots:** All entries in a column directly below a pivot entry are zero.

$$\mathbf{\rho(A) = \text{Total Number of Non-Zero Rows in Row Echelon Form}}$$

#### Invariance of Rank under Elementary Row Transformations:
Elementary row transformations do not alter the linear span of the row vectors:
1. Interchanging two rows: $R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$ (Permutation matrix $P$, $|P| = -1 \ne 0$).
2. Multiplying a row by a non-zero scalar: $R_i \to k R_i, \; k \ne 0$ (Diagonal scaling matrix $D$, $|D| = k \ne 0$).
3. Adding a scalar multiple of one row to another: $R_i \to R_i + c R_j$ (Shear matrix $S$, $|S| = 1 \ne 0$).
Because all elementary matrices are non-singular ($|E| \ne 0$), rank is strictly invariant: $\rho(E A) = \rho(A)$.

---

### 3. Deep-Dive: Normal (Canonical) Form $[I_r\ 0; 0\ 0]$

By using **both elementary row AND elementary column operations**, any arbitrary $m \times n$ matrix $A$ of rank $r$ can be brought into its unique Canonical Normal Form:

$$\mathbf{N = \begin{bmatrix} I_r & 0_{r \times (n-r)} \\ 0_{(m-r) \times r} & 0_{(m-r) \times (n-r)} \end{bmatrix}}$$

#### Existence of Non-Singular Matrices $P$ and $Q$:
There exist non-singular square matrices $P$ (of order $m$) and $Q$ (of order $n$) representing the product of all row operations and column operations respectively, such that:
$$\mathbf{P \cdot A \cdot Q = \begin{bmatrix} I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}}$$
- **Procedure to compute $P$ and $Q$ simultaneously:**
  1. Write $A_{m \times n} = I_m \cdot A \cdot I_n$.
  2. Perform elementary row operations on $A$ and simultaneously on the pre-factor $I_m$.
  3. Perform elementary column operations on $A$ and simultaneously on the post-factor $I_n$.
  4. When the central matrix reduces to $[I_r\ 0; 0\ 0]$, the left matrix becomes $P$ and the right matrix becomes $Q$!

### 4. Fundamental Properties & Sylvester's Rank Inequalities

1. **Sub-multiplicative Property:** $\rho(A B) \le \min(\rho(A), \rho(B))$.
2. **Sylvester's Law of Nullity:** For an $m \times n$ matrix $A$ and $n \times p$ matrix $B$:
   $$\mathbf{\rho(A) + \rho(B) - n \le \rho(A B) \le \min(\rho(A), \rho(B))}$$
3. **Rank of Transpose & Conjugate:** $\rho(A^T) = \rho(A)$ and $\rho(A^T A) = \rho(A A^T) = \rho(A)$.
4. **Rank of Sum:** $\rho(A + B) \le \rho(A) + \rho(B)$.

**Problem:**
Find the rank of matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 3 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Compute the Determinant of $A$**
$$|A| = (2)(6) - (4)(3) = 12 - 12 = 0$$
Since $|A| = 0$, the rank cannot be 2: $\rho(A) < 2$.

**Step 2 ⚡: Check Order-1 Minors (Single Entries)**
The matrix contains non-zero entries (e.g., $a_{11} = 2 \ne 0$). Therefore, there is at least one non-vanishing minor of order 1.

**Step 3 🎯: Row Echelon Verification ($R_2 \to R_2 - 1.5R_1$)**
$$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 3 & 6 \end{bmatrix} \sim \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
There is exactly **1 non-zero row**.

🎯 Rank of $A$: $\mathbf{\rho(A) = 1}$.

**University Question (4 Marks):**
Reduce the matrix $A$ to Row Echelon form and determine its rank:
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 7 \\ 3 & 6 & 10 \end{bmatrix}$$

---

**Step 1 🚀: Eliminate entries below pivot in Column 1**
Apply row operations $R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1$ and $R_3 \to R_3 - 3R_1$:
$$A \sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Eliminate entry below pivot in Column 3**
Apply $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$:
$$A \sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Step 3 🎯: Count Non-Zero Rows**
The echelon form contains 2 non-zero rows ($[1, 2, 3]$ and $[0, 0, 1]$) and 1 zero row.

🎯 Row Echelon Rank: $\mathbf{\rho(A) = 2}$.

**University Question (6 Marks - MMDU Annual Exam):**
Reduce matrix $A$ to its Normal Form $\begin{bmatrix} I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ using elementary row AND column transformations, and find non-singular matrices $P$ and $Q$ such that $PAQ = \begin{bmatrix} I_r & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$:
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

---

**Step 1 🚀: Setup Identity Matrices $A = I_3 A I_3$**
$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} A \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Elementary Row Operation $R_2 \to R_2 - R_1$**
$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} A I_3$$

**Step 3 🎯: Elementary Column Operations $C_2 \to C_2 - C_1, \; C_3 \to C_3 - 2C_1$**
$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} A \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Step 4 🏁: Row Operation $R_3 \to R_3 - R_2$ and Column Operation $C_3 \to C_3 - C_2$**
$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} A \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
This is the standard Normal Form $\begin{bmatrix} I_2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.
