### 1. Vector Differential Calculus: Del, Gradient, Divergence & Curl

> 🟢 **The Vector Differential Operator Del ($\nabla$):**
> $$\mathbf{\nabla = \hat{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x} + \hat{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial y} + \hat{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}}$$

#### A. Gradient of a Scalar Field ($\nabla\phi$) & Directional Derivative:
- **Gradient:** $\mathbf{\nabla\phi = \hat{i}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x} + \hat{j}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y} + \hat{k}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}}$ (normal to level surface $\phi(x,y,z) = c$).
- **Unit Normal Vector:** $\mathbf{\hat{n} = \frac{\nabla\phi}{|\nabla\phi|}}$.
- **Directional Derivative along $\vec{a}$:** $\mathbf{\text{D.D.} = \nabla\phi \cdot \frac{\vec{a}}{|\vec{a}|}}$ (maximum magnitude $= |\nabla\phi|$ in direction of $\nabla\phi$).

#### B. Divergence ($\nabla \cdot \vec{F}$) & Curl ($\nabla \times \vec{F}$):
- **Divergence (Scalar):** $\mathbf{\text{div}\,\vec{F} = \nabla \cdot \vec{F} = \frac{\partial F_1}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial z}}$.
  - **Solenoidal Field:** $\mathbf{\nabla \cdot \vec{F} = 0}$.
- **Curl (Vector):** $\mathbf{\text{curl}\,\vec{F} = \nabla \times \vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ F_1 & F_2 & F_3 \end{vmatrix}}$.
  - **Irrotational (Conservative) Field:** $\mathbf{\nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0} \iff \vec{F} = \nabla\phi}$.

**University Question (6 Marks - MMDU Question Paper):**
Find the directional derivative of $\phi = x^2 y z + 4 x z^2$ at point $P(1, -2, -1)$ in the direction of vector $\vec{a} = 2\hat{i} - \hat{j} - 2\hat{k}$.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Calculate $\nabla\phi$ at $P(1, -2, -1)$**
$$\nabla\phi = (2xyz + 4z^2)\hat{i} + (x^2 z)\hat{j} + (x^2 y + 8xz)\hat{k} \Big|_{(1,-2,-1)} = 8\hat{i} - \hat{j} - 10\hat{k}$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Project onto Unit Vector $\hat{a}$**
$$\hat{a} = \frac{2\hat{i} - \hat{j} - 2\hat{k}}{\sqrt{4 + 1 + 4}} = \frac{2\hat{i} - \hat{j} - 2\hat{k}}{3} \implies \text{D.D.} = \nabla\phi \cdot \hat{a} = \frac{16 + 1 + 20}{3} = \frac{37}{3}$$

<div class="example-answer-box">
🎯 Directional Derivative: $\mathbf{\frac{37}{3} \approx 12.33}$.
</div>

**University Question (6 Marks - MMDU Question Paper):**
Prove that $\vec{F} = (y^2 \cos x + z^3)\hat{i} + (2y \sin x - 4)\hat{j} + (3x z^2 + 2)\hat{k}$ is irrotational, and find its scalar potential $\phi$.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Evaluate $\text{curl}\,\vec{F} = \nabla \times \vec{F}$**
$$\nabla \times \vec{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ \frac{\partial}{\partial x} & \frac{\partial}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial}{\partial z} \\ y^2 \cos x + z^3 & 2y \sin x - 4 & 3xz^2 + 2 \end{vmatrix} = (0)\hat{i} - (3z^2 - 3z^2)\hat{j} + (2y\cos x - 2y\cos x)\hat{k} = \vec{0}$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Integrate Exact Differential $d\phi = \vec{F} \cdot d\vec{r}$**
$$d\phi = d(y^2 \sin x) + d(x z^3) - d(4y) + d(2z) \implies \phi = y^2 \sin x + x z^3 - 4y + 2z + C$$

<div class="example-answer-box">
🎯 $\nabla \times \vec{F} = \vec{0}$ (Irrotational) | Scalar Potential: $\mathbf{\phi = y^2 \sin x + x z^3 - 4y + 2z + C}$.
</div>
