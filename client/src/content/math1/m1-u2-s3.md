### 1. Rigorous Analysis of Indeterminate Forms & L'Hospital's Rule

#### What is an Indeterminate Form?
An algebraic limit expression $\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)}$ is called **indeterminate** if substituting the limiting point $x = a$ fails to yield a well-defined value because the limiting behaviors of the numerator and denominator conflict.

#### The 7 Classic Indeterminate Forms:
$$\mathbf{\frac{0}{0}, \quad \frac{\infty}{\infty}, \quad 0 \times \infty, \quad \infty - \infty, \quad 0^0, \quad 1^\infty, \quad \infty^0}$$

---

### 2. L'Hospital's Rule Theorem & Derivation

> 🟢 **Theorem (Bernoulli & L'Hospital):** Let $f$ and $g$ be differentiable on an open interval containing $a$ (except possibly at $a$). If $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = 0$ and $\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = 0$ (or both tend to $\pm\infty$), and $g'(x) \ne 0$ near $a$, then:
> $$\mathbf{\lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f'(x)}{g'(x)}}$$

---

### 3. Systematic Reduction Strategies for Non-Fractional Forms

1. **Form $0 \times \infty$:** Invert one factor into the denominator: $f(x) \cdot g(x) = \frac{f(x)}{1/g(x)} \implies \frac{0}{0}$.
2. **Form $\infty - \infty$:** Combine over a common denominator using LCM or rationalization.
3. **Exponential Forms ($0^0, 1^\infty, \infty^0$):** Let $y = [f(x)]^{g(x)}$, take $\ln y = g(x) \ln[f(x)]$, evaluate $L = \lim_{x \to a} \ln y$, and recover $\lim y = e^L$.

**University Question (4 Marks):**
Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \left( \frac{1}{x^2} - \frac{1}{\sin^2 x} \right)$.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Convert $\infty - \infty$ into $\frac{0}{0}$ via LCM**
$$\lim_{x \to 0} \left( \frac{\sin^2 x - x^2}{x^2 \sin^2 x} \right) = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin^2 x - x^2}{x^4} \cdot \lim_{x \to 0} \left( \frac{x}{\sin x} \right)^2 = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sin^2 x - x^2}{x^4}$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Use Series Expansion $\sin x = x - \frac{x^3}{6} + \mathcal{O}(x^5)$**
$$\sin^2 x - x^2 = \left(x - \frac{x^3}{6}\right)^2 - x^2 = -\frac{x^4}{3} + \mathcal{O}(x^6)$$
$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{-\frac{x^4}{3} + \mathcal{O}(x^6)}{x^4} = -\frac{1}{3}$$

<div class="example-answer-box">
🎯 Evaluated Limit: $\mathbf{-\frac{1}{3}}$.
</div>

**University Question (6 Marks - MMDU Question Paper):**
Evaluate $\lim_{x \to 0} \left( \frac{\tan x}{x} \right)^{1/x^2}$.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Identify $1^\infty$ Form & Take Natural Logarithm**
Let $y = \lim_{x \to 0} \left( \frac{\tan x}{x} \right)^{1/x^2} \implies \ln y = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\ln\left( \frac{\tan x}{x} \right)}{x^2}$.

**Step 2 ⚡: Series Expansion of $\tan x$**
$$\frac{\tan x}{x} = 1 + \frac{x^2}{3} + \mathcal{O}(x^4) \implies \ln\left( \frac{\tan x}{x} \right) = \frac{x^2}{3} + \mathcal{O}(x^4)$$

**Step 3 🎯: Evaluate Limit and Exponentiate**
$$\ln y = \lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\frac{x^2}{3} + \mathcal{O}(x^4)}{x^2} = \frac{1}{3} \implies y = e^{1/3} = \sqrt[3]{e}$$

<div class="example-answer-box">
🎯 Limit: $\mathbf{e^{1/3} = \sqrt[3]{e}}$.
</div>
