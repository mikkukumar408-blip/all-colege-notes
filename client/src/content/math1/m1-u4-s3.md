### 1. Fourier Series: Foundations & Euler's Formulas

#### Fourier Representation in $(-\pi, \pi)$ or $(0, 2\pi)$:
$$\mathbf{f(x) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left[ a_n \cos(nx) + b_n \sin(nx) \right]}$$
$$\mathbf{a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^\pi f(x) \, dx, \quad a_n = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^\pi f(x) \cos(nx) \, dx, \quad b_n = \frac{1}{\pi} \int_{-\pi}^\pi f(x) \sin(nx) \, dx}$$

#### Dirichlet's Conditions & Jump Discontinuity:
At any finite jump discontinuity $x = c$, the Fourier series converges to the average of the left and right limits:
$$\mathbf{S(c) = \frac{f(c^+) + f(c^-)}{2}}$$

**University Question (6 Marks - MMDU Question Paper):**
Obtain the Fourier series for $f(x) = x^2$ in $(-\pi, \pi)$. Hence deduce $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Symmetry & Coefficients**
Since $f(-x) = (-x)^2 = x^2 = f(x)$, $f(x)$ is **even** $\implies b_n = 0$.
$$a_0 = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_0^\pi x^2 \, dx = \frac{2\pi^2}{3}, \quad a_n = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_0^\pi x^2 \cos(nx) \, dx = \frac{4(-1)^n}{n^2}$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Fourier Expansion & Deduction at $x = \pi$**
$$x^2 = \frac{\pi^2}{3} + 4 \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n}{n^2} \cos(nx) \implies \text{At } x = \pi: \; \pi^2 = \frac{\pi^2}{3} + 4\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} \implies \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$$
