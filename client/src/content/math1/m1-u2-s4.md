### 1. Rigorous Theory of Beta and Gamma Special Functions

#### A. The Gamma Function (Euler's Integral of Second Kind):
> 🟢 **Definition:** For any real number $n > 0$, the **Gamma Function** $\Gamma(n)$ is defined as:
> $$\mathbf{\Gamma(n) = \int_0^\infty x^{n-1} e^{-x} \, dx \quad (n > 0)}$$

#### Fundamental Properties of $\Gamma(n)$:
1. **Recurrence Relation:** $\mathbf{\Gamma(n + 1) = n \, \Gamma(n)}$ and $\mathbf{\Gamma(n + 1) = n!}$ for positive integers.
2. **Base & Half-Integer Values:** $\Gamma(1) = 1$ and $\mathbf{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = \sqrt{\pi}}$.

---

### 2. The Beta Function (Euler's Integral of First Kind)

> 🟢 **Definition:** For $m > 0, n > 0$, the **Beta Function** $B(m, n)$ is defined as:
> $$\mathbf{B(m, n) = \int_0^1 x^{m-1} (1 - x)^{n-1} \, dx}$$

#### Key Properties and Master Identities:
1. **Symmetry:** $\mathbf{B(m, n) = B(n, m)}$.
2. **Trigonometric Form:**
   $$\mathbf{B(m, n) = 2 \int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^{2m-1}\theta \cos^{2n-1}\theta \, d\theta}$$
3. **Infinite Domain Form:**
   $$\mathbf{B(m, n) = \int_0^\infty \frac{t^{m-1}}{(1 + t)^{m+n}} \, dt}$$
4. **Beta-Gamma Bridge Relation:**
   $$\mathbf{B(m, n) = \frac{\Gamma(m) \cdot \Gamma(n)}{\Gamma(m + n)}}$$
5. **Walli's / Trigonometric Integral Formula:**
   $$\mathbf{\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^p\theta \cos^q\theta \, d\theta = \frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{2}\right) \Gamma\left(\frac{q+1}{2}\right)}{2 \, \Gamma\left(\frac{p+q+2}{2}\right)}}$$
6. **Legendre's Duplication Formula:**
   $$\mathbf{\Gamma(m) \, \Gamma\left(m + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2^{2m-1}} \, \Gamma(2m)}$$

**University Question (6 Marks - MMDU Question Paper):**
Evaluate $\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^4\theta \cos^6\theta \, d\theta$ using Beta and Gamma functions, and prove $\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}} = \frac{\sqrt{\pi} \, \Gamma(1/4)}{4 \, \Gamma(3/4)}$.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Evaluate Trigonometric Integral ($p = 4, q = 6$)**
$$I_1 = \frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{5}{2}\right) \Gamma\left(\frac{7}{2}\right)}{2 \, \Gamma(6)} = \frac{\left(\frac{3\sqrt{\pi}}{4}\right) \left(\frac{15\sqrt{\pi}}{8}\right)}{2 \times 120} = \frac{3\pi}{512}$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Prove $\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{\sqrt{1 - x^4}} = \frac{\sqrt{\pi} \, \Gamma(1/4)}{4 \, \Gamma(3/4)}$**
Substitute $x^4 = u \implies x = u^{1/4}, \; dx = \frac{1}{4} u^{-3/4} du$:
$$I_2 = \frac{1}{4} \int_0^1 u^{1/4 - 1} (1 - u)^{1/2 - 1} du = \frac{1}{4} B\left(\frac{1}{4}, \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{\Gamma(1/4) \Gamma(1/2)}{4 \, \Gamma(3/4)} = \frac{\sqrt{\pi} \, \Gamma(1/4)}{4 \, \Gamma(3/4)}$$
