### 1. Cauchy's Root Test, Alternating Series & Leibnitz's Rule

#### A. Cauchy's $n^{\text{th}}$ Root Test:
> 🟢 **Theorem:** Let $\sum u_n$ be a positive-term series and evaluate $\mathbf{L = \lim_{n \to \infty} (u_n)^{1/n}}$:
> - $\mathbf{L < 1} \implies$ **Convergent** | $\mathbf{L > 1} \implies$ **Divergent** | $\mathbf{L = 1} \implies$ **Test Fails**.

#### B. Alternating Series & Leibnitz's Rule:
> 🟢 **Theorem:** An alternating series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^{n-1} u_n = u_1 - u_2 + u_3 - u_4 + \dots$ ($u_n > 0$) converges if:
> 1. **Monotonic Decrease:** $u_{n+1} \le u_n$ for all $n$.
> 2. **Vanishing Limit:** $\lim_{n \to \infty} u_n = 0$.

#### C. Absolute vs Conditional Convergence:
- **Absolutely Convergent:** $\sum |u_n|$ converges (e.g., $\sum \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^2}$).
- **Conditionally Convergent:** $\sum u_n$ converges via Leibnitz's test, but $\sum |u_n|$ diverges (e.g., $\sum \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}$ and $\sum \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{\sqrt{n}}$).

---

**University Question (4 Marks - MMDU Question Paper):**
Test the alternating series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{\sqrt{n}}$ for absolute and conditional convergence.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Verify Leibnitz's Conditions**
Here $u_n = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} > 0$. Since $\sqrt{n+1} > \sqrt{n} \implies u_{n+1} < u_n$, and $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}} = 0$, the series **converges** by Leibnitz's Rule.

**Step 2 ⚡: Check Absolute Series $\sum |u_n|$**
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty |u_n| = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^{1/2}}$$
Since $p = \frac{1}{2} \le 1$, $\sum |u_n|$ **diverges**. Therefore, the series is **Conditionally Convergent**.

<div class="example-answer-box">
🎯 $\sum \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{\sqrt{n}}$ is **Conditionally Convergent** | $\sum \frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n^2}$ is **Absolutely Convergent**.
