### 1. The Gauss-Jordan Inversion Algorithm (Theoretical Analysis)

> 📌 **University Exam Distinction:** Never use the classical Adjugate formula $A^{-1} = \frac{\text{adj}(A)}{|A|}$ when the question explicitly states **"Find inverse using Gauss-Jordan method"**! The classical method requires $O(n!)$ operations via minors, whereas Gauss-Jordan requires only $O(n^3)$ row operations.

#### Mathematical Principle of Gauss-Jordan Inversion:
Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ invertible matrix ($|A| \ne 0$). If a sequence of elementary row operations $E_1, E_2, \dots, E_k$ transforms $A$ into the identity matrix $I$:
$$(E_k \dots E_2 E_1) A = I \implies \mathbf{A^{-1} = E_k \dots E_2 E_1}$$
Therefore, if we apply the identical sequence of operations to the augmented matrix $[ A \mid I_n ]$:
$$(E_k \dots E_2 E_1) [ A \mid I_n ] = [ (E_k \dots E_1) A \mid (E_k \dots E_1) I_n ] = \mathbf{[ I_n \mid A^{-1} ]}$$

---

### 2. Step-by-Step Algorithmic Execution Pipeline

1. **Augmentation:** Construct the partitioned block matrix $[ A \mid I_n ]$ of dimension $n \times 2n$.
2. **Forward Elimination (Upper Triangularization):**
   - For column $j = 1, 2, \dots, n$:
     - **Pivoting:** Find row $i \ge j$ with a non-zero pivot $a_{ij}$ (preferring $1$ or $-1$). Swap $R_j \leftrightarrow R_i$.
     - If pivot entry $a_{jj} = 0$, matrix is **singular** ($A^{-1}$ does not exist!).
     - Normalize the pivot row: $R_j \to \frac{1}{a_{jj}} R_j$.
     - Eliminate all entries below pivot: for each $k > j$, $R_k \to R_k - a_{kj} R_j$.
3. **Backward Elimination (Diagonalization):**
   - Starting from the bottom row $j = n, n-1, \dots, 2$:
     - For each row $k < j$, eliminate the above-pivot entries: $R_k \to R_k - a_{kj} R_j$.
4. **Extraction:** The right half of the augmented matrix is strictly $A^{-1}$. Verify that $A \cdot A^{-1} = I_n$.

---

### 3. Comparison of Matrix Inversion Techniques

| Feature | Gauss-Jordan Method | Adjugate Formula | LU Decomposition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Operation Count** | $n^3$ additions & multiplications | $\sim (n+1)!$ determinant operations | $\frac{2}{3}n^3$ operations |
| **Memory Required** | $n \times 2n$ augmented grid | High cofactor matrix cache | $n \times n$ in-place factorization |
| **Suitability** | Hand calculation in university exams | $2 \times 2$ matrices only | High-performance computer algorithms |

**Problem:**
Find the inverse of matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ using the Gauss-Jordan method.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Setup Augmented Matrix $[A \mid I]$**
$$[ A \mid I ] = \left[ \begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 4 & 0 & 1 \end{array} \right]$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Make entry below pivot zero ($R_2 \to R_2 - 3R_1$)**
$$\sim \left[ \begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 & -3 & 1 \end{array} \right]$$

**Step 3 🎯: Normalize pivot of row 2 ($R_2 \to -\frac{1}{2}R_2$)**
$$\sim \left[ \begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{3}{2} & -\frac{1}{2} \end{array} \right]$$

**Step 4 🏁: Eliminate entry above pivot ($R_1 \to R_1 - 2R_2$)**
$$\sim \left[ \begin{array}{cc|cc} 1 & 0 & -2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{3}{2} & -\frac{1}{2} \end{array} \right]$$

🎯 Final Inverse: $\mathbf{A^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 1 \\ 1.5 & -0.5 \end{bmatrix}}$. Verified: $A \cdot A^{-1} = I$.

**University Question (6 Marks - MMDU Question Paper):**
Employ the Gauss-Jordan method to determine the inverse of:
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

---

**Step 1 🚀: Swap Rows to Get Leading Pivot 1 ($R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$)**
$$[A \mid I] = \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|ccc} 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{array} \right]$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Eliminate Column 1 ($R_2 \to R_2 - 2R_1, \; R_3 \to R_3 - R_1$)**
$$\sim \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|ccc} 1 & 2 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -3 & -1 & 1 & -2 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 1 & 0 & -1 & 1 \end{array} \right]$$

**Step 3 🎯: Swap $R_2 \leftrightarrow R_3$, Normalize and Eliminate**
$$\begin{aligned}
R_2 &\to -R_3: & \left[0, 1, -1 \mathrel{\Big|} 0, 1, -1\right] \\
R_3 &\to R_3 + 3R_2: & \left[0, 0, -4 \mathrel{\Big|} 1, 1, -3\right] \\
R_3 &\to -\frac{1}{4}R_3: & \left[0, 0, 1 \mathrel{\Big|} -\frac{1}{4}, -\frac{1}{4}, \frac{3}{4}\right]
\end{aligned}$$

**Step 4 🏁: Eliminate Above Pivots to reach $[I \mid A^{-1}]$**
$$\begin{aligned}
R_2 &\to R_2 + R_3: & \left[0, 1, 0 \mathrel{\Big|} -\frac{1}{4}, \frac{3}{4}, -\frac{1}{4}\right] \\
R_1 &\to R_1 - R_3 - 2R_2: & \left[1, 0, 0 \mathrel{\Big|} \frac{3}{4}, -\frac{1}{4}, -\frac{1}{4}\right]
\end{aligned}$$

<div class="example-answer-box">
🎯 Complete Inverse: $\mathbf{A^{-1} = \frac{1}{4} \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -1 & -1 \\ -1 & 3 & -1 \\ -1 & -1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}}$. Verified: $|A| = 4 \ne 0$.
