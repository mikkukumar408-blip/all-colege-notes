### 1. Comprehensive Theory: System of Linear Equations $AX = B$

A system of $m$ linear equations in $n$ unknowns $x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n$ is represented in matrix notation as:
$$\begin{bmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} & \dots & a_{1n} \\ a_{21} & a_{22} & \dots & a_{2n} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ a_{m1} & a_{m2} & \dots & a_{mn} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \\ x_n \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \\ \vdots \\ b_m \end{bmatrix} \iff \mathbf{A X = B}$$

#### The Rouché–Capelli Theorem:
> 🟢 **Theorem Statement:** A system of linear equations $A X = B$ is **consistent** (possesses at least one solution) if and only if the rank of the coefficient matrix $A$ equals the rank of the augmented matrix $[A \mid B]$:
> $$\mathbf{\rho(A) = \rho([A \mid B])}$$

---

### 2. Complete Classification of Solution Regimes

```
                                  [ System AX = B ]
                                          |
                 +------------------------+------------------------+
                 |                                                 |
         rho(A) != rho([A|B])                              rho(A) == rho([A|B])
                 |                                                 |
         [ INCONSISTENT ]                                   [ CONSISTENT ]
         Zero Solutions!                                           |
         (Hyperplanes parallel                             +-------+-------+
          or non-intersecting)                             |               |
                                                      rho(A) == n     rho(A) = r < n
                                                           |               |
                                                   [ UNIQUE SOLN ]  [ INFINITE SOLNS ]
                                                   Single point     (n - r) free parameters
                                                   intersection     Forms an affine subspace
```

---

### 3. Homogeneous Linear Systems $AX = 0$

For $AX = 0$, $B = 0$. Since augmenting a column of zeros cannot increase matrix rank, $\rho(A) = \rho([A \mid 0])$ always holds. Therefore, **a homogeneous system is ALWAYS consistent** and always possesses at least the trivial solution $X = 0$.

#### Nature of Solutions for Square Systems ($n \times n$):
1. **Trivial Solution ($X = 0$):**
   - Occurs when $\rho(A) = n \iff |A| \ne 0$ (Non-singular matrix).
   - The columns of $A$ are linearly independent; the only solution is $x_1 = x_2 = \dots = x_n = 0$.
2. **Non-Trivial Solutions (Infinite Non-Zero Solutions):**
   - Occurs when $\rho(A) = r < n \iff |A| = 0$ (Singular matrix), yielding $(n - r)$ linearly independent parameters.

> 🔴 **Examiner's Trap:** When an exam problem asks: *"Find values of $k$ for which the homogeneous equations have non-trivial (non-zero) solutions"*, simply set the determinant to zero: $\mathbf{|A| = 0}$!

**University Question (6 Marks - Classic MMDU Exam Problem):**
Investigate for what values of $\lambda$ and $\mu$ the system of equations:
$$\begin{cases} x + y + z = 6 \\ x + 2y + 3z = 10 \\ x + 2y + \lambda z = \mu \end{cases}$$
has: (i) No solution, (ii) A unique solution, (iii) Infinitely many solutions.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Setup Augmented Matrix and Echelon Reduction**
$$[A \mid B] = \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 & 10 \\ 1 & 2 & \lambda & \mu \end{array} \right] \xrightarrow{\substack{R_2 \to R_2 - R_1 \\ R_3 \to R_3 - R_1}} \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 0 & 1 & \lambda - 1 & \mu - 6 \end{array} \right]$$

$$\xrightarrow{R_3 \to R_3 - R_2} \left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 1 & 1 & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & \lambda - 3 & \mu - 10 \end{array} \right]$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Case Analysis by Rouché–Capelli Theorem**
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{1. Case (i) No Solution (Inconsistent): } & \lambda = 3, \; \mu \ne 10 \implies \rho(A) = 2 \ne \rho([A \mid B]) = 3 \\
\text{2. Case (ii) Unique Solution: } & \lambda \ne 3, \; \forall \mu \implies \rho(A) = \rho([A \mid B]) = 3 = n \\
\text{3. Case (iii) Infinite Solutions: } & \lambda = 3, \; \mu = 10 \implies \rho(A) = \rho([A \mid B]) = 2 < n \; (z = k)
\end{aligned}$$

<div class="example-answer-box">
🎯 **Verified Result:** (i) No solution for $\lambda = 3, \mu \ne 10$ | (ii) Unique for $\lambda \ne 3$ | (iii) Infinite for $\lambda = 3, \mu = 10$.
</div>
