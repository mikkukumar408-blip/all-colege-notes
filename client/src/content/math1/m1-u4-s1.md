### 1. In-Depth Theory of Infinite Series Convergence & Divergence

Let $\sum_{n=1}^\infty u_n = u_1 + u_2 + \dots + u_n + \dots$ be an infinite series with partial sums $S_k = \sum_{n=1}^k u_n$.
- **Necessary Condition ($n^{\text{th}}$ Term Test):** If $\sum u_n$ converges, then $\mathbf{\lim_{n \to \infty} u_n = 0}$. If $\lim_{n \to \infty} u_n \ne 0$, the series **strictly diverges**.
- **Geometric Series $\sum r^n$:** Converges iff $|r| < 1$.
- **Harmonic $p$-Series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^p}$:** Converges strictly if $\mathbf{p > 1}$, and diverges if $\mathbf{p \le 1}$.

---

### 2. Systematic Positive-Term Convergence Tests Pipeline

1. **Limit Comparison Test:** If $\lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{u_n}{v_n} = l$ ($0 < l < \infty$, finite and non-zero), then $\sum u_n$ and $\sum v_n$ converge or diverge together.
2. **D'Alembert's Ratio Test:** Let $\mathbf{L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{u_n}{u_{n+1}}}$:
   - $\mathbf{L > 1} \implies$ **Convergent** | $\mathbf{L < 1} \implies$ **Divergent** | $\mathbf{L = 1} \implies$ **Test Fails** (use Raabe's or Logarithmic test).
3. **Raabe's Test (when $L = 1$):** Evaluate $\mathbf{R = \lim_{n \to \infty} n\left(\frac{u_n}{u_{n+1}} - 1\right)}$:
   - $\mathbf{R > 1} \implies$ **Convergent** | $\mathbf{R < 1} \implies$ **Divergent** | $\mathbf{R = 1} \implies$ **Test Fails**.
4. **Logarithmic Test (when $L = 1$ with exponents/$e$):** Evaluate $\mathbf{L' = \lim_{n \to \infty} n \ln\left(\frac{u_n}{u_{n+1}}\right)}$:
   - $\mathbf{L' > 1} \implies$ **Convergent** | $\mathbf{L' < 1} \implies$ **Divergent**.

**University Question (6 Marks - Classic MMDU Exam Problem):**
Examine the convergence of the series:
$$\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1 \cdot 3}{2 \cdot 4} + \frac{1 \cdot 3 \cdot 5}{2 \cdot 4 \cdot 6} + \dots + \frac{1 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \dots (2n-1)}{2 \cdot 4 \cdot 6 \dots (2n)} + \dots$$

---

**Step 1 🚀: Ratio Test Setup**
Here $\frac{u_n}{u_{n+1}} = \frac{2n+2}{2n+1} \implies L = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{2n+2}{2n+1} = 1 \implies$ **Ratio Test Fails!**

**Step 2 ⚡: Apply Raabe's Test**
$$R = \lim_{n \to \infty} n \left( \frac{2n+2}{2n+1} - 1 \right) = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{n}{2n+1} = \frac{1}{2} < 1$$

<div class="example-answer-box">
🎯 Ratio test fails ($L=1$); Raabe's Test yields $R = \frac{1}{2} < 1 \implies$ Series is **Divergent**.
