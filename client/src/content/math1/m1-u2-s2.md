### 1. Curvature, Radius & Center of Curvature: Comprehensive Theory

#### Physical Concept of Curvature ($\kappa$):
> 🟢 **Definition:** Let $P$ and $Q$ be two neighboring points on a smooth curve separated by arc length $\Delta s$. If the tangent vectors at $P$ and $Q$ make angles $\psi$ and $\psi + \Delta\psi$ with the positive $x$-axis, the **Curvature** $\kappa$ is the rate of turning of the tangent line per unit arc length:
> $$\mathbf{\kappa = \lim_{\Delta s \to 0} \left| \frac{\Delta\psi}{\Delta s} \right| = \left| \frac{d\psi}{ds} \right|}$$

#### Radius of Curvature ($\rho$):
The Radius of Curvature $\rho$ is the reciprocal of the curvature:
$$\mathbf{\rho = \frac{1}{\kappa} = \left| \frac{ds}{d\psi} \right|}$$
- A straight line has zero bending $\implies \kappa = 0 \implies \rho = \infty$.
- A circle of radius $R$ bends uniformly $\implies \kappa = 1/R \implies \rho = R$.

### 2. Analytical Formulas for Radius of Curvature $\rho$

| Coordinate Representation | Curve Equation | Radius of Curvature Formula $\rho$ |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cartesian $y = f(x)$** | $y = f(x)$ | $\mathbf{\rho = \frac{\left(1 + y_1^2\right)^{3/2}}{y_2}} \quad \left(y_1 = \frac{dy}{dx}, \; y_2 = \frac{d^2y}{dx^2}\right)$ |
| **Cartesian $x = g(y)$** | $x = g(y)$ | $\mathbf{\rho = \frac{\left(1 + x_1^2\right)^{3/2}}{x_2}} \quad \left(x_1 = \frac{dx}{dy}, \; x_2 = \frac{d^2x}{dy^2}\right)$ |
| **Parametric** | $x = x(t), \; y = y(t)$ | $\mathbf{\rho = \frac{\left(x'^2 + y'^2\right)^{3/2}}{x' y'' - y' x''}}$ |
| **Polar Form** | $r = f(\theta)$ | $\mathbf{\rho = \frac{\left(r^2 + r_1^2\right)^{3/2}}{r^2 + 2r_1^2 - r r_2}} \quad \left(r_1 = \frac{dr}{d\theta}, \; r_2 = \frac{d^2r}{d\theta^2}\right)$ |
| **Pedal Form ($p$-$r$)** | $p = f(r)$ | $\mathbf{\rho = r \frac{dr}{dp}}$ |

### 3. Center of Curvature $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$ and Chord of Curvature

The circle that shares the same tangent and curvature as the curve at point $P(x, y)$ is the **Osculating Circle (Circle of Curvature)**. Its center $C(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$ is given by:
$$\mathbf{\bar{x} = x - \frac{y_1(1 + y_1^2)}{y_2}}, \quad \mathbf{\bar{y} = y + \frac{1 + y_1^2}{y_2}}$$
- **Equation of Circle of Curvature:** $(x - \bar{x})^2 + (y - \bar{y})^2 = \rho^2$.
- **Evolute:** The locus of the centers of curvature of all points on a curve is called the **Evolute** of that curve.

#### Chords of Curvature:
- **Chord parallel to $x$-axis:** $C_x = 2 \rho \sin\psi = \mathbf{\frac{2 y_1 (1 + y_1^2)}{y_2}}$
- **Chord parallel to $y$-axis:** $C_y = 2 \rho \cos\psi = \mathbf{\frac{2 (1 + y_1^2)}{y_2}}$
- **Chord through the Pole (Polar Curve $r = f(\theta)$):** $C_p = 2 \rho \sin\phi = \mathbf{\frac{2r(r^2 + r_1^2)}{r^2 + 2r_1^2 - r r_2}}$

**University Question (6 Marks):**
Find the radius of curvature at any point $t$ on the cycloid $x = a(t + \sin t), \; y = a(1 - \cos t)$.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Compute First and Second Derivatives w.r.t $t$**
- $x' = \frac{dx}{dt} = a(1 + \cos t) = 2a \cos^2\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)$
- $y' = \frac{dy}{dt} = a \sin t = 2a \sin\left(\frac{t}{2}\right) \cos\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)$
- $x'' = -a \sin t, \quad y'' = a \cos t$

**Step 2 ⚡: Denominator $x' y'' - y' x''$ and Numerator $(x'^2 + y'^2)^{3/2}$**
$$x' y'' - y' x'' = a^2(1 + \cos t) = 2a^2 \cos^2\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)$$
$$(x'^2 + y'^2)^{3/2} = \left[4a^2 \cos^2\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)\right]^{3/2} = 8a^3 \cos^3\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)$$
$$\rho = \frac{8a^3 \cos^3(t/2)}{2a^2 \cos^2(t/2)} = 4a \cos\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)$$

<div class="example-answer-box">
🎯 Radius of Curvature: $\mathbf{\rho = 4a \cos\left(\frac{t}{2}\right)}$.
</div>

**University Question (6 Marks - MMDU Question Paper):**
Find the coordinates of the centre of curvature $(\bar{X}, \bar{Y})$ and the equation of the evolute of the parabola $y^2 = 4ax$.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Derivatives for $y^2 = 4ax$**
$$2y y_1 = 4a \implies y_1 = \frac{2a}{y}, \quad y_2 = -\frac{4a^2}{y^3}$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Centre of Curvature Formula**
$$\bar{X} = x - \frac{y_1(1 + y_1^2)}{y_2} = x + \frac{y^2 + 4a^2}{2a} = 3x + 2a$$
$$\bar{Y} = y + \frac{1 + y_1^2}{y_2} = -\frac{y^3}{4a^2}$$

**Step 3 🎯: Eliminate $(x, y)$ to Obtain the Evolute Equation**
Substitute $x = \frac{\bar{X} - 2a}{3}$ and $y^2 = (4a^2 \bar{Y})^{2/3}$ into $y^2 = 4ax$:
$$27 a \bar{Y}^2 = 4 (\bar{X} - 2a)^3$$

<div class="example-answer-box">
🎯 Centre of Curvature: $(\bar{X}, \bar{Y}) = \left(3x + 2a, \, -\frac{y^3}{4a^2}\right)$ | Evolute: $\mathbf{27a Y^2 = 4(X - 2a)^3}$.
</div>
