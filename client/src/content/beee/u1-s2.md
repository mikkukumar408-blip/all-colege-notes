### 1. In-Depth Theoretical Foundations of Kirchhoff's Laws

German physicist Gustav Kirchhoff established the fundamental topological constraints governing all lumped electrical circuits.

#### A. Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) — Conservation of Electric Charge:
> 🟢 **Formal Statement:** The algebraic sum of currents entering any closed boundary or electrical node is identically zero at every instant of time:
> $$\mathbf{\sum_{k=1}^N i_k(t) = 0 \iff \sum i_{\text{entering}} = \sum i_{\text{leaving}}}$$

- **Physical Derivation:** By Maxwell's continuity equation $∇ · \vec{J} + \frac{∂ ρ_v}{∂ t} = 0$. Integrating over a closed Gaussian surface enclosing the node:
  $$\oint_S \vec{J} · d\vec{A} = -\frac{d}{dt} \int_V ρ_v , dV = -\frac{dq_{\text{enclosed}}}{dt}$$
  Since an electrical node has zero physical volume ($V → 0$), it cannot store or accumulate net charge: $\frac{dq}{dt} = 0 ⇒ \sum i_k = 0$.

#### B. Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) — Conservation of Energy:
> 🟢 **Formal Statement:** The algebraic sum of all branch voltages around any closed loop or mesh in an electric network is identically zero:
> $$\mathbf{\sum_{k=1}^M v_k(t) = 0 \iff \sum V_{\text{rises}} = \sum V_{\text{drops}}}$$

- **Physical Derivation:** In static and quasi-static electromagnetic fields, Faraday's Law states $\oint_C \vec{E} · d\vec{l} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$. In lumped circuits, magnetic flux linking the loop is confined inside discrete magnetic elements (inductors), so outside the elements $\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = 0 ⇒ \oint_C \vec{E} · d\vec{l} = 0$, confirming potential is a conservative field.

### 2. Network Topology & Systematic Circuit Formulation

Let a planar network contain $b$ branches and $n$ nodes:
1. **Independent KCL Node Equations:** Exactly $\mathbf{n - 1}$ independent equations (one node chosen as reference / ground).
2. **Independent KVL Mesh Equations:** By Euler's topological theorem:
   $$\mathbf{l = b - n + 1}    \text{independent fundamental loops (meshes)}$$

#### Matrix Formulation of Mesh Analysis:
$$[R] [I] = [V]$$
$$\begin{bmatrix} R_{11} & R_{12} & ... & R_{1m} \\ R_{21} & R_{22} & ... & R_{2m} \\ ⋮ & ⋮ & ⋱ & ⋮ \\ R_{m1} & R_{m2} & ... & R_{mm} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} I₁ \\ I₂ \\ ⋮ \\ I_m \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} V_{s1} \\ V_{s2} \\ ⋮ \\ V_{sm} \end{bmatrix}$$
- **Self-resistance $R_{ii}$:** Sum of all resistances in mesh $i$ (always positive).
- **Mutual resistance $R_{ij}$ ($i ≠ j$):** Negative sum of resistances shared between mesh $i$ and mesh $j$ (symmetric: $R_{ij} = R_{ji}$).
- **Driving voltage $V_{si}$:** Net algebraic sum of voltage rises clockwise around mesh $i$.

$$30 - 4I_1 - 2(I₁ - I₂) = 0 ⇒ 6I_1 - 2I_2 = 30    \text{--- (Eq 1)}$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Write KVL for Mesh 2**
$$-2(I₂ - I₁) - 6I_2 - 20 = 0 ⇒ -2I_1 + 8I_2 = -20    \text{--- (Eq 2)}$$

**Step 3 🎯: Solve Simultaneously**
Multiply Eq 2 by 3: $-6I_1 + 24I_2 = -60$.
Add to Eq 1:
$$22I_2 = -30 ⇒ \mathbf{I₂ = -1.364~\text{A}}$$
Substitute $I₂$ into Eq 1:
$$6I_1 - 2(-1.364) = 30 ⇒ 6I_1 = 27.272 ⇒ \mathbf{I₁ = 4.545~\text{A}}$$


🎯 Mesh Currents: $\mathbf{I₁ = 4.55~\text{A}}$ (clockwise), $\mathbf{I₂ = -1.36~\text{A}}$ (counter-clockwise).

**Step 1 🚀: Evaluate Main Determinant $\Delta$**
$$\Delta = 8[(9)(12) - (-3)(-3)] - (-2)[(-2)(12) - (-3)(-4)] + (-4)[(-2)(-3) - (9)(-4)]$$
$$= 8[108 - 9] + 2[-24 - 12] - 4[6 + 36] = 8(99) + 2(-36) - 4(42)$$
$$= 792 - 72 - 168 = \mathbf{552}$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Evaluate $\Delta_1$ for $I₁$**
Replace column 1 with $[24, 0, 0]^T$:
$$\Delta_1 = 24[(9)(12) - (-3)(-3)] = 24(99) = \mathbf{2376}$$
$$I₁ = \frac{\Delta_1}{\Delta} = \frac{2376}{552} = \mathbf{4.304~\text{Amperes}}$$

**Step 3 🎯: Evaluate $\Delta_3$ for $I_3$**
Replace column 3 with $[24, 0, 0]^T$:
$$\Delta_3 = 24[(-2)(-3) - (9)(-4)] = 24(6 + 36) = 24(42) = \mathbf{1008}$$
$$I_3 = \frac{\Delta_3}{\Delta} = \frac{1008}{552} = \mathbf{1.826~\text{Amperes}}$$

**Step 4 🏁: Branch Current**
$$I_{4\Omega} = I₁ - I_3 = 4.304 - 1.826 = \mathbf{2.478~\text{Amperes}}$$
