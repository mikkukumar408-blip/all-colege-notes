### 1. DC Machines: Construction, Action & Hand Rules

A **Direct Current Machine** is an electromechanical energy converter operating bidirectionally:
- **DC Generator:** Mechanical shaft torque $→$ DC electrical power via Faraday's Law.
- **DC Motor:** DC electrical power $→$ Mechanical shaft torque via Lorentz Force Law.

#### Structural Components:
1. **Stator:** Yoke (magnetic frame), Field poles with pole shoes, Shunt/Series Field windings.
2. **Rotor (Armature):** Laminated cylindrical silicon-steel core, insulated armature conductors in slots.
3. **Commutator:** Split-ring copper wedge segments insulated with mica, acting as a mechanical rectifier/inverter.
4. **Brushes:** High-grade carbon blocks riding on the commutator to collect or inject current.

### 3. Mathematical Derivations of DC Machine Equations

#### A. General Induced EMF Equation:
Let:
- $P$ = Number of poles, $\phi$ = Magnetic flux per pole ($\text{Wb}$)
- $Z$ = Total number of armature conductors, $N$ = Rotational speed ($\text{rpm}$)
- $A$ = Number of parallel paths ($A = 2$ for Wave winding; $A = P$ for Lap winding)

Flux cut by one conductor in 1 revolution: $d\phi = P \phi$.
Time for 1 revolution: $dt = \frac{60}{N}~\text{seconds}$.
EMF induced in one conductor: $e = \frac{d\phi}{dt} = \frac{P \phi}{60 / N} = \frac{P \phi N}{60}~\text{Volts}$.
Since $Z$ conductors are divided into $A$ parallel paths, conductors in series per path = $Z / A$:
$$\mathbf{E_g = \left(\frac{P \phi N}{60}\right) · \frac{Z}{A} = \frac{P \phi Z N}{60 A}}$$

#### B. DC Motor Back EMF ($E_b$) and Voltage Balance:
$$\mathbf{V = E_b + I_a R_a} ⇒ \mathbf{I_a = \frac{V - E_b}{R_a}}$$
$$\mathbf{E_b = \frac{P \phi Z N}{60 A}}$$

#### C. Electromagnetic Torque Equation:
Mechanical gross power developed equals electromagnetic power:
$$P_{\text{mech}} = T_e · \omega = T_e \left(\frac{2π N}{60}\right) = E_b · I_a$$
$$T_e \left(\frac{2π N}{60}\right) = \left(\frac{P \phi Z N}{60 A}\right) I_a$$
$$\mathbf{T_e = \frac{1}{2π} \left(\frac{P Z}{A}\right) \phi I_a ≈ 0.159 \left(\frac{P Z}{A}\right) \phi I_a    [\text{N}·\text{m}]}$$
$$\mathbf{T_e \propto \phi · I_a}$$

**University Question (8 Marks):**
A 2$40~\text{V}$ DC shunt motor has armature resistance Rₐ = 0.4~\Omega and field resistance Rₛₕ = 120~\Omega.
1. What is the armature current at the instant of starting (when N = 0)?
2. What is the total line current when running at rated speed with Eb = 228,V?

---

**Step 1 🚀: Starting Armature Current (Eb = 0)**
Iₐ,ₛₜₐᵣₜ = (V - 0 / Rₐ) = (240 / 0.4) = 600.0,Amperes!
*(Demonstrates why a 3-point starter resistor is mandatory!)*

**Step 2 ⚡: Running Armature Current**
Iₐ = (V - Eb / Rₐ) = (240 - 228 / 0.4) = (12 / 0.4) = 30.0,Amperes

**Step 3 🎯: Total Line Current IL = Iₐ + Iₛₕ**
Iₛₕ = (V / Rₛₕ) = \frac{240}{120} = 2.0,A
IL = 30.0 + 2.0 = 32.0,Amperes


🎯 Starting Current = $600.0~\text{A}$ (Dangerous without starter) | Normal Running Line Current = $32.0~\text{A}$.




**University Question (10 Marks):**
A 230,V DC shunt motor runs at 1000,pm while taking an armature current of 40,A. The armature resistance is 0.25~\Omega.
If the mechanical torque is reduced by 50% and magnetic flux per pole remains constant, find the new operating speed.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Initial Back EMF Eb₁**
Eb₁ = V - Iₐ₁ Rₐ = 230 - (40 × 0.25) = 230 - 10 = 220.0,V

**Step 2 ⚡: New Armature Current for 50% Torque**
Since torque T \propto φ Iₐ and flux φ is constant:
T₂ = 0.5 T₁ ⇒ Iₐ₂ = 0.5 Iₐ₁ = 0.5 × 40 = 20.0,A

**Step 3 🎯: New Back EMF Eb₂**
Eb₂ = V - Iₐ₂ Rₐ = 230 - (20 × 0.25) = 230 - 5 = 225.0,V

**Step 4 🏁: Speed Relation (Eb \propto N)**
$$\frac{N_2}{N_1} = \frac{E_{b2}}{E_{b1}} \implies N_2 = N_1 \left(\frac{E_{b2}}{E_{b1}}\right) = 1000 \cdot \left(\frac{225}{220}\right) = 1000 \cdot 1.0227 = 1022.7\text{ rpm}$$


🎯 New Operating Speed: $\mathbf{N_2 = 1023~\text{rpm}}$ (Speed rises slightly as load lightens).
