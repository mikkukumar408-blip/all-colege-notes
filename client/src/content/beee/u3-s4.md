### 1. Bipolar Junction Transistors (BJT): Physical Structure & Working

A **Bipolar Junction Transistor** is a three-terminal, two-junction semiconductor device whose operation relies on transport of both majority and minority charge carriers (bipolar).

#### The Three Structural Regions:
1. **Emitter (E):** Heavily doped to inject maximum majority carriers into the base. Moderate physical size.
2. **Base (B):** Ultra-thin ($< 1,μ\text{m}$) and extremely lightly doped to minimize electron-hole recombination ($< 2% - 5%$).
3. **Collector (C):** Moderately doped, physically the largest region to safely dissipate Joule heat generated by collecting carriers.

---

### 2. The Three Operating Regimes & Biasing Configurations

| Operating Region | <pill blue>Emitter-Base Junction (EBJ)</pill> | <pill pink>Collector-Base Junction (CBJ)</pill> | <pill green>Circuit Application</pill> |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Active Region** | **Forward Biased** | **Reverse Biased** | Linear Analog Amplifier |
| **Saturation Region** | **Forward Biased** | **Forward Biased** | Digital Switch ON (Closed) ($V_{CE~\text{sat}} ≈ 0.2~\text{V}$) |
| **Cutoff Region** | **Reverse Biased** | **Reverse Biased** | Digital Switch OFF (Open) ($I_C ≈ 0$) |
| **Inverse Active** | Reverse Biased | Forward Biased | Attenuator (Rarely used) |

---

### 3. Current Gain Relations ($\alpha, \beta, \gamma$) & Characteristics

1. **Current Continuity Equation:**
   $$\mathbf{I_E = I_B + I_C}$$
2. **Common-Base Current Gain ($\alpha$):**
   $$\mathbf{\alpha = \frac{I_C}{I_E} ≈ 0.95 - 0.998}$$
3. **Common-Emitter Current Gain ($\beta$ or $h_{FE}$):**
   $$\mathbf{\beta = \frac{I_C}{I_B} ≈ 50 - 300}$$
4. **Mathematical Derivation of $\beta$ in terms of $\alpha$:**
   $$I_E = I_B + I_C ⇒ \frac{I_E}{I_C} = \frac{I_B}{I_C} + 1 ⇒ \frac{1}{\alpha} = \frac{1}{\beta} + 1$$
   $$\frac{1}{\beta} = \frac{1}{\alpha} - 1 = \frac{1 - \alpha}{\alpha} ⇒ \mathbf{\beta = \frac{\alpha}{1 - \alpha}},      \mathbf{\alpha = \frac{\beta}{\beta + 1}}$$

#### DC Operating Point (Q-Point) and Load Line:
Applying KVL to the collector-emitter loop of a Common-Emitter amplifier:
$$\mathbf{V_{CE} = V_{CC} - I_C R_C}$$
- Upper intercept (Saturation limit): $V_{CE} = 0 ⇒ I_{C~\text{sat}} = \frac{V_{CC}}{R_C}$.
- Lower intercept (Cutoff limit): $I_C = 0 ⇒ V_{CE~\text{cutoff}} = V_{CC}$.

**Problem:**
A transistor has a base current $I_B = 50,μ\text{A}$ and collector current $I_C = 4.95~\text{mA}$. Find: (1) Emitter current $I_E$, (2) Current gain $\beta$, (3) Current gain $\alpha$.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Emitter Current $I_E = I_C + I_B$**
$$I_E = 4.95~\text{mA} + 0.050~\text{mA} = \mathbf{5.0~\text{mA}}$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Current Gain $\beta$**
$$\beta = \frac{I_C}{I_B} = \frac{4.95 \times 10^{-3}}{50 \times 10^{-6}} = \mathbf{99.0}$$

**Step 3 🎯: Current Gain $\alpha$**
$$\alpha = \frac{\beta}{\beta + 1} = \frac{99}{100} = \mathbf{0.99}$$


🎯 $I_E = 5.0~\text{mA}$ | Common-Emitter Gain $\beta = 99$ | Common-Base Gain $\alpha = 0.99$.

**University Question (12 Marks - University Exam):**
Design a voltage-divider biased Common-Emitter amplifier stage with VCC = 15,V, quiescent point IC = 2.0,mA, VCE = 7.5,V, and stability factor condition VE = 0.1 VCC. Transistor has β = 100, VBE = 0.7,V. Find RE, RC, R₁, R₂.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Determine RE and RC**
VE = 0.1 VCC = 0.1(15) = 1.5,V
RE = (VE / IE) ≈ (VE / IC) = (1.5,V / 2.0,mA) = 750~\Omega
From collector loop: VCC = VCE + IC RC + VE:
15 = 7.5 + (2.0 × 10⁻³) RC + 1.5 ⇒ 2.0 × 10⁻³ RC = 6.0 ⇒ RC = 3.0,k\Omega

**Step 2 ⚡: Base Voltage VB**
VB = VE + VBE = 1.5 + 0.7 = 2.2,Volts

**Step 3 🎯: Size Bias Resistors R₁ and R₂**
For excellent thermal stability, make divider current I₁ ≈ 10 IB:
IB = (IC / β) = (2.0,mA / 100) = 20,μA ⇒ Idᵢᵥ = 10 IB = 200,μA
R₂ = (VB / Idᵢᵥ) = (2.2,V / 200,μA) = 11.0,k\Omega
R₁ = (VCC - VB / Idᵢᵥ) = (15 - 2.2 / 200,μA) = (12.8 / 200,μA) = 64.0,k\Omega


🎯 Designed Bias Stage: $R_C = 3.0~\text{k}\Omega, R_E = 750~\Omega, R₁ = 64~\text{k}\Omega, R₂ = 11~\text{k}\Omega$.
