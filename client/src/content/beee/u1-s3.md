### 1. In-Depth Analysis of Thevenin's and Norton's Network Theorems

Network reduction theorems replace complex multi-loop linear networks with simple two-terminal equivalent generators.

#### A. Thevenin's Theorem (1883):
> 🟢 **Formal Theorem Statement:** Any linear two-terminal bilateral active network containing independent or dependent sources and linear resistors can be replaced at its external terminals $A-B$ by an equivalent circuit consisting of a single independent ideal voltage source $V_{\text{th}}$ in series with an equivalent internal resistance $R_{\text{th}}$.

- **Thevenin Voltage ($V_{\text{th}}$):** The open-circuit voltage appearing across terminals $A-B$ when the load resistor $R_L$ is disconnected:
  $$V_{\text{th}} = V_{\text{oc}} = V_A - V_B    (\text{with } R_L → ∞)$$
- **Thevenin Resistance ($R_{\text{th}}$):** The resistance measured looking back into terminals $A-B$ with all internal independent sources deactivated:
  - Independent Voltage Sources $→$ **Short-Circuited** ($V = 0~\text{V}$, bare conducting wire).
  - Independent Current Sources $→$ **Open-Circuited** ($I = 0~\text{A}$, open cut branch).
  - **Networks containing Dependent Sources:** You cannot simply combine resistors! Instead, apply an external test source $V_{\text{test}} = 1.0~\text{V}$ across terminals $A-B$, calculate resulting test current $I_{\text{test}}$, and evaluate:
    $$R_{\text{th}} = \frac{V_{\text{test}}}{I_{\text{test}}} = \frac{V_{\text{oc}}}{I_{\text{sc}}}$$

#### B. Norton's Theorem (Dual Generator Representation):
Dual of Thevenin's Theorem: Replaces the active network with an ideal current generator $I_N$ connected in parallel with Norton resistance $R_N$:
$$I_N = I_{\text{sc}}    (\text{short-circuit current flowing between terminals } A \text{ and } B),      R_N = R_{\text{th}}$$
$$\text{Source Transformation Duality:}    V_{\text{th}} = I_N · R_{\text{th}} \iff I_N = \frac{V_{\text{th}}}{R_{\text{th}}}$$

### 2. Maximum Power Transfer Theorem (MPTT) Analytical Derivation


**University Theorem Statement:**
*In any linear bilateral DC electrical network delivering energy to a variable load resistor $R_L$, the active power delivered to the load is maximum when the load resistance equals the internal Thevenin resistance of the driving network looking back from the load terminals:*
$$\boxed{R_L = R_{\text{th}}}$$

---

**Step 1 🚀: Circuit Modeling & Mathematical Expression for Load Power $P_L$**
Connecting an adjustable load resistor $R_L$ across the Thevenin equivalent terminals $A-B$ yields a single-loop series circuit:
- By Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL) and Ohm's Law, the loop current is:
  $$I_L = \frac{V_{\text{th}}}{R_{\text{th}} + R_L}$$
- The active Joule electrical power dissipated in the load resistor is:
  $$P_L = I_L^2 · R_L = \left(\frac{V_{\text{th}}}{R_{\text{th}} + R_L}\right)^2 R_L = \frac{V_{\text{th}}^2 · R_L}{(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^2}$$

---

**Step 2 ⚡: Differential Calculus Optimization (Quotient Rule Derivation)**
To find the load resistance $R_L$ that maximizes $P_L$, we treat $P_L$ as a continuous function of the independent variable $R_L$ while $V_{\text{th}}$ and $R_{\text{th}}$ remain constant. Applying the Quotient Rule of differential calculus $\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{u}{v}\right] = \frac{u'v - uv'}{v^2}$:
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{dP_L}{dR_L} &= V_{\text{th}}^2 \cdot \frac{d}{dR_L}\left[ \frac{R_L}{(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^2} \right] \\
&= V_{\text{th}}^2 \cdot \left[ \frac{1 \cdot (R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^2 - R_L \cdot 2(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)}{(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^4} \right] \\
&= V_{\text{th}}^2 \cdot \left[ \frac{(R_{\text{th}} + R_L) \cdot \left\{ (R_{\text{th}} + R_L) - 2R_L \right\}}{(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^4} \right] \\
&= V_{\text{th}}^2 \cdot \frac{R_{\text{th}} - R_L}{(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^3}
\end{aligned}$$

---

**Step 3 🎯: First Derivative Test (Condition for Stationary Point)**
Setting the first derivative to zero to identify the critical point:
$$\frac{dP_L}{dR_L} = 0 \iff V_{\text{th}}^2 · \frac{R_{\text{th}} - R_L}{(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^3} = 0$$
Since $V_{\text{th}} ≠ 0$ and $(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^3 ≠ 0$, the numerator must vanish identically:
$$R_{\text{th}} - R_L = 0 ⇒ \boxed{R_L = R_{\text{th}}}$$

---

**Step 4 🔍: Rigorous Second Derivative Verification ($\frac{d^2P_L}{dR_L^2} < 0$)**
To mathematically guarantee that $R_L = R_{\text{th}}$ corresponds to a **global maximum** and not a minimum or inflection point:
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^2P_L}{dR_L^2} &= V_{\text{th}}^2 · \frac{d}{dR_L}\left[ \frac{R_{\text{th}} - R_L}{(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^3} \right] \\ 
&= V_{\text{th}}^2 · \left[ \frac{(-1)(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^3 - (R_{\text{th}} - R_L) · 3(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^2}{(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^6} \right] \\ 
&= V_{\text{th}}^2 · \left[ \frac{-(R_{\text{th}} + R_L) - 3(R_{\text{th}} - R_L)}{(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^4} \right] = V_{\text{th}}^2 · \frac{2R_L - 4R_{\text{th}}}{(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^4}
\end{aligned}$$
Evaluating the second derivative specifically at the critical point $R_L = R_{\text{th}}$:
$$\left. \frac{d^2P_L}{dR_L^2} \right|_{R_L = R_{\text{th}}} = V_{\text{th}}^2 · \frac{2R_{\text{th}} - 4R_{\text{th}}}{(2R_{\text{th}})^4} = -\frac{2 V_{\text{th}}^2 R_{\text{th}}}{16 R_{\text{th}}^4} = -\frac{V_{\text{th}}^2}{8 R_{\text{th}}^3} < 0$$
Because $V_{\text{th}} > 0$ and $R_{\text{th}} > 0$, the second derivative is strictly negative. This **rigorously proves that power $P_L$ reaches a global maximum** when $R_L = R_{\text{th}}$!

---

**Step 5 🏁: Maximum Power Delivered to Load**
Substituting $R_L = R_{\text{th}}$ back into the load power formulation:
$$\boxed{P_{L,\max} = \left. \frac{V_{\text{th}}^2 · R_L}{(R_{\text{th}} + R_L)^2} \right|_{R_L = R_{\text{th}}} = \frac{V_{\text{th}}^2 · R_{\text{th}}}{(2R_{\text{th}})^2} = \frac{V_{\text{th}}^2}{4 R_{\text{th}}}}$$

**Step 6 ⚡: Power Transfer Efficiency Formulation ($\eta = 50%$)**
At maximum power transfer condition:
- Total electrical power generated by the Thevenin voltage source:
  $$P_{\text{source}} = I_L · V_{\text{th}} = \left(\frac{V_{\text{th}}}{2R_{\text{th}}}\right) V_{\text{th}} = \frac{V_{\text{th}}^2}{2 R_{\text{th}}}$$
- Internal Joule heat dissipated inside Thevenin resistance $R_{\text{th}}$:
  $$P_{\text{internal}} = I_L^2 · R_{\text{th}} = \left(\frac{V_{\text{th}}}{2R_{\text{th}}}\right)^2 R_{\text{th}} = \frac{V_{\text{th}}^2}{4 R_{\text{th}}}$$
- Transmission Efficiency ($\eta$):
  $$\eta = \frac{P_{L,\max}}{P_{\text{source}}} \times 100% = \frac{\frac{V_{\text{th}}^2}{4 R_{\text{th}}}}{\frac{V_{\text{th}}^2}{2 R_{\text{th}}}} \times 100% = \boxed{50%}$$
The remaining $50%$ of generated power is inevitably burned as Joule waste heat inside the internal resistance $R_{\text{th}}$.

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Objective** | **Maximum Signal Power Transfer** ($R_L = R_{\text{th}}$) | **Maximum Transmission Efficiency** ($\eta > 95%$, $R_L \gg R_{\text{th}}$) |

**Step 1 🚀: Find Open-Circuit Voltage $V_{\text{oc}} = V_{\text{th}}$**
With terminals A-B open:
- Loop current: $i_x = \frac{10 - 2i_x}{4 + 6} ⇒ 10 i_x = 10 - 2i_x ⇒ 12 i_x = 10 ⇒ i_x = \frac{5}{6}~\text{A}$.
- Output voltage across $6~\Omega$:
  $$V_{\text{th}} = i_x \times 6 = \frac{5}{6} \times 6 = \mathbf{5.0~\text{Volts}}$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Find $R_{\text{th}}$ using Test Source Method ($V_{\text{test}} = 1~\text{V}$)**
Deactivate independent source ($10~\text{V} → 0~\text{V}$ short circuit). Keep dependent source $2i_x$ active!
Apply test source $V_{\text{test}} = 1.0~\text{V}$ at terminals A-B:
- From node equation: $i_x$ flows in reverse: $i_x = -\frac{V_{\text{test}} + 2i_x}{4} ⇒ 4i_x = -1 - 2i_x ⇒ 6i_x = -1 ⇒ i_x = -1/6~\text{A}$.
- Current from test generator:
  $$I_{\text{test}} = \frac{V_{\text{test}}}{6} - i_x = \frac{1}{6} - \left(-\frac{1}{6}\right) = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}~\text{A}$$
- Looking-back resistance:
  $R_{\text{th}} = \frac{V_{\text{test}}}{I_{\text{test}}} = \frac{1.0}{1/3} = \mathbf{3.0~\Omega}$
