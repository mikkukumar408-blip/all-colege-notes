### 1. The P-N Junction: Formation, Depletion Region & Electrostatics

When P-type and N-type semiconductors are metallurgically joined in a single crystal:
1. **Diffusion Current:** High hole concentration in P-region causes holes to diffuse into N-region; electrons diffuse from N-region into P-region.
2. **Uncovered Ion Cores:** Diffusing electrons and holes recombine, leaving unneutralized donor ions ($N_D^+$) on the N-side and unneutralized acceptor ions ($N_A^-$) on the P-side.
3. **The Depletion Layer (Space Charge Region):** A thin boundary region ($W ≈ 0.1 - 1,μ\text{m}$) completely devoid of mobile charge carriers.
4. **Built-In Potential Barrier ($V_0$ / $V_{bi}$):** The space charge establishes an internal electric field $\vec{E}$ pointing from N to P that opposes further diffusion:
   $$\mathbf{V_0 = V_T \ln\left(\frac{N_A · N_D}{n_i^2}\right)}$$
   where $V_T = \frac{k_B T}{q} ≈ 26~\text{mV}$ at $300~\text{K}$.
   - For Silicon: $V_0 ≈ 0.7~\text{Volts}$. For Germanium: $V_0 ≈ 0.3~\text{Volts}$.

---

### 2. Biasing Regimes & The Shockley Diode Equation

```
Forward Bias (+ to P, - to N)              Reverse Bias (- to P, + to N)
Vₐpplied opposes V₀                      Vₐpplied aids V₀
Depletion width shrinks: W -> 0            Depletion width widens: W >> W₀
Current surges exponentially:              Current drops to tiny reverse saturation:
ID = Iₛ * [ exp\frac{VD}{eta*VT} - 1 ]     ID = -Iₛ (nano-amperes)
```

#### Shockley Diode Characteristic Equation:
$$\mathbf{I_D = I_s \left( e^{\frac{V_D}{\eta V_T}} - 1 \right)}$$
- $I_s$: Reverse saturation current ($~ 10^{-12}~\text{A}$ for Si, $~ 10^{-6}~\text{A}$ for Ge).
- $\eta$: Ideality factor ($\eta = 1$ for Ge, $\eta ≈ 1 - 2$ for Si).

---

### 3. Zener Breakdown vs Avalanche Breakdown & Regulators

When reverse bias voltage reaches critical breakdown threshold $V_{BR}$:
1. **Zener Breakdown ($V_{BR} < 6~\text{V}$):** Occurs in **heavily doped junctions** with ultra-thin depletion width ($< 10~\text{nm}$). High internal field ($> 10^6~\text{V/cm}$) directly pulls electrons out of covalent bonds via **quantum mechanical tunneling**. Has a **negative temperature coefficient**.
2. **Avalanche Breakdown ($V_{BR} > 6~\text{V}$):** Occurs in **lightly doped junctions** with wide depletion layers. High electric field accelerates minority carriers to huge kinetic energies, knocking free secondary electrons via **impact ionization** in a chain reaction. Has a **positive temperature coefficient**.

#### Zener Voltage Regulator Operation:
When biased in the breakdown region, voltage across the Zener stays fixed at $V_Z$ regardless of current variations ($I_{Z,\min} ≤ I_Z ≤ I_{Z,\max}$):
$$\mathbf{R_s = \frac{V_{in} - V_Z}{I_Z + I_L}}$$

**Problem:**
A silicon diode with knee voltage $V_0 = 0.7~\text{V}$ is connected in series with a resistor $R = 220~\Omega$ across a $5.0~\text{V}$ DC supply. Find the diode current $I_D$ and voltage across the resistor $V_R$.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Check Diode State**
Input supply $5.0~\text{V} > 0.7~\text{V}$, so the diode is **Forward Biased**.

**Step 2 ⚡: Resistor Voltage Drop**
$$V_R = V_s - V_D = 5.0 - 0.7 = \mathbf{4.3~\text{Volts}}$$

**Step 3 🎯: Diode Current**
$$I_D = \frac{V_R}{R} = \frac{4.3~\text{V}}{220~\Omega} = \mathbf{0.0195~\text{A} = 19.55~\text{mA}}$$


🎯 Resistor Voltage $V_R = 4.3~\text{V}$ | Forward Current $I_D = 19.55~\text{mA}$.

**University Question (10 Marks):**
A silicon diode has reverse saturation current Iₛ = 10⁻¹²,A and ideality factor η = 1.
1. Calculate the diode current at a forward bias of VD = 0.65,V at T = 300,K (VT = 26,mV).
2. Determine the dynamic (AC) resistance rd at this operating point.

---

**Step 1 🚀: Apply Shockley Equation**
$$I_D = I_s \left( e^{V_D / (eta V_T)} - 1 \right)$$
$$\frac{V_D}{V_T} = \frac{0.65}{0.026} = 25.0$$
$$I_D = 10^{-12} \left( e^{25} - 1 \right) = 10^{-12} (7.20 \times 10^{10}) = 0.0720\text{ A} = 72.0\text{ mA}$$

**Step 2 ⚡: Calculate Dynamic Resistance rd**
rd = (η VT / ID) = (0.026,V / 0.072,A) = 0.361~\Omega


🎯 Forward Current $I_D = 72.0~\text{mA}$ | Dynamic Resistance $r_d = 0.361~\Omega$.
